What is AI, in plain English?

AI (artificial intelligence) is technology that lets computers do things that normally require human thinking, like understanding language, recognizing images, making predictions, or generating new text, images, or compute code.

Most AI you interact with today (like ChatGPT or Claude) works by learning patterns from huge amounts of data. Instead of a human writing out "if X happens, do Y" for every possible case, the system is trained on examples until it can recognize patterns and generate reasonable responses to new situations it hasn't seen before.

A few ways to think about it:

  • Narrow AI — built for one specific task (spam filters, recommendation engines, voice assistants). This is most AI in the world today.
  • Generative AI — creates new content (text, images, audio, video) based on a prompt. ChatGPT, Claude, Midjourney, etc.
  • General AI (AGI) — a hypothetical system that could match human-level thinking across any domain. Doesn't exist yet, despite the hype.

What we know about AI today.

The "smart" feeling comes from scale: massive datasets, huge computing power, and statistical pattern-matching — not the machine actually "understanding" things the way a person does. It's closer to an extremely sophisticated autosuggest or pattern-recognizer than a digital brain, even when the output feels remarkably human.

Worth remembering: AI is moving fast, and it's easy to feel left behind. The smartest move right now is treating it as a resource and adopting new platforms early to save time and money — while it's still cheap and accessible. That window won't stay open forever.

Bottom line

AI isn't magic, and it isn't going away. It's a tool and like any tool, the earlier you pick it up, the more it pays off. Start small. Start now.
