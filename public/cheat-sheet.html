5 Things You Can Ask AI Today

For people who didn't grow up with a cell phone — including business owners. No jargon, guaranteed.
AI Free for Boomers
aifreeforboomers.com
Getting there: open claude.ai or chatgpt.com in your web browser (the same place you type website names). Make a free account. Type in the box, press Enter. That's it — you're using AI.
1
Check if something is a scam
Suspicious email, text, or phone message? Don't guess — paste it in and ask. AI is very good at spotting the tricks.
Say this: “I received this message. Is it a scam? Explain how you can tell.” (then paste the message)
2
Write anything in half the time
A tricky email, a sympathy card, a complaint letter, a reply to a customer. Tell it who it's for and the tone you want.
Say this: “Write a warm, short reply to this email from my customer. Keep it friendly but professional.” (paste the email)
3
Get anything explained — without feeling dumb
Medicare paperwork, a lease clause, a diagnosis your doctor rushed through, what a QR code even is. Ask as many follow-ups as you want — it never gets impatient.
Say this: “Explain this in plain English, step by step. I'm smart, just new to it.” (paste or describe the thing)
4
Do your business's busywork for owners
Facebook posts, sale announcements, job listings, review replies, even a price-increase letter. Describe your business once — it does the rest.
Say this: “I own a [type of business] in [town]. Write a Facebook post announcing [your news]. Sound like a real person, not an ad.”
5
Make something you've always wanted to make
Don't just ask AI questions — build with it. Your memoir, a family cookbook, a new service for your business, the newsletter you've been putting off. You bring the life experience; it brings the elbow grease.
Say this: “I've always wanted to make [your idea]. Help me build it — start by asking what you need to know, then let's do the first piece today.”
Three rules that keep you safe
Never type in passwords, your Social Security number, or bank details. It doesn't need them — and nothing legitimate ever asks.
Double-check facts. AI is occasionally confidently wrong. For anything medical, legal, or financial, verify before acting.
You can't break it. There is no wrong question and no button that ruins anything. Experiment freely.
